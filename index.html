01 / The question

What if an organism with no brain could teach us how to connect a city?

Optimization sounds like a computer problem. Physarum shows another route: grow everywhere at first, sense what is useful, then invest more material in the connections that work.

This site follows the story in your presentation, with the heavy mathematics kept in the downloadable paper.

02 / The organism

Three simple behaviours
make computation possible.

Physarum is a plasmodial slime mould. Its body is a changing network of tubes, not a brain making plans.

01

It explores

Thin branches spread into several directions to sample the surrounding space.

02

It senses food

Oat flakes act as nutrient sources. Chemical signals and cytoplasmic flow guide growth.

03

It reinforces

Useful routes thicken. Weak branches receive less flow and gradually fade away.

“The result is a near-optimal network without a central controller.”

04 / Urban planning

Mumbai Metro,
grown as a network.

Food sources are placed at major transit hubs. The simulated slime explores Mumbai's geography, then highlights the strongest rail corridors.

Food Node (Station) Primary Rail Corridor Dissolved Tendril

The five stages

  1. 01 / Place sources Churchgate, CST, Dadar, Kurla, Thane & Panvel become oat flakes.
  2. 02 / Explore Exploratory tendrils radiate outward into all surrounding terrain.
  3. 03 / Reinforce High-traffic corridors (Western, Central & Harbour) grow brighter.
  4. 04 / Prune Redundant or unhelpful branches dissolve away.
  5. 05 / Optimise A resilient, compact transport network remains.
